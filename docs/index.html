The idea

Horizon is designed as an evidence pipeline, not a location toy. Acquisition produces observations; persistence preserves them; analysis and UI consume what was actually observed.

What makes it different

Session-scoped sequencing, durable staging, transactional draining, typed provenance, atomic exports, and explicit separation of raw / normalized / derived / model-level data.

Technical anatomy

Horizon treats positioning and orbital context as different layers of the same evidence chain. A measured observation is preserved with its measurement epoch and provenance; navigation state such as PVT, clock bias, and clock drift remains distinct from orbital prediction. On the orbital side, catalog identity can feed TLE / OMM sources into SGP4 / SDP4 propagation, including deep-space propagation, producing an explicit state vector and orbital state rather than an opaque coordinate.

Reference frames remain explicit: TEME, ECI, and ECEF are treated as coordinate frames with meaning, not as interchangeable labels. Observation geometry can then be expressed through Azimuth / Elevation, Range / Range Rate, and signal observables such as Doppler and C/N₀ where the underlying measurement is actually available. UTC / GNSS Time anchors the timeline so observed and derived states can be compared without hiding their different time domains.

Visibility is a derivation, not an assumption

Pass prediction and visibility belong downstream of the evidence boundary. Horizon can keep the difference between what a receiver measured and what an orbital model predicts explicit—so an apparent pass, a calculated ephemeris, and a real received signal are never silently collapsed into the same fact.

Evidence boundary

The repository records the physical-device verification boundary explicitly. A missing device receipt is never upgraded into a VERIFIED claim.
